## What the gate measures

Spec §5–§7 / §9: the **full commit round trip**. A leader appends payload
messages into its log buffer; the sender agent MDC-fans MTU datagrams to two
followers; each follower rebuilds the contiguous stream position-addressed into
its own log buffer and its archive agent block-records + `fdatasync`es the
rebuilt bytes, advancing its **durable** counter; on each durable advance the
follower emits an **AppendPosition** control datagram back to the leader; the
leader's receiver demuxes those, the sender ranks them as an order statistic
(the k-th largest durable position across the quorum, k = majority) and advances
the leader's **commit** counter; the leader gossips **CommitPosition** forward so
followers learn the commit frontier. The metric is:

- **committed/s** — messages whose position is ≤ the leader's committed
  (quorum-durable) frontier, per second; and
- **p50 / p99 commit latency** — wall time from a message's append to the moment
  the commit counter passes its position.

Every committed message is **quorum-fsync'd** (leader + at least one follower
made it durable) — commit is a real durability statement, not an ack count.
The leader's log buffer is heap-backed (no writeback); each node's journal lives
on the "real disk" (here ext4 `/dev/sda1`; on the fleet a local NVMe volume).

## Methodology

- **Drain-inclusive single clock.** The headline `committed/s` = `messages /
  full_wall_seconds` where the **one** wall clock starts before the appender
  begins and stops only after the leader's **commit** counter reaches the stream
  end — i.e. *after* every appended byte is quorum-fsync'd. This is the M1 gate's
  accounting lesson (see `docs/benchmarks/uc2-m1-gate-2026-07-09.md`, "Accounting
  correction"): dividing a byte total captured *after* the drain by the *append
  window* time inflates the number by drained-backlog fsync time that is absent
  from the denominator. Here the drain seconds are in the denominator by
  construction.
- **Sampled commit latency.** Every 1024th append records `(position,
  Instant::now())`; the pair is resolved (latency pushed) when the commit counter
  passes that position. p50/p99/max are computed over those samples. Sampling
  (rather than timing every message) keeps the appender hot-path allocation-free
  at the sample rate while still yielding thousands of points over a 10 s run.
- **Admission window vs commit (spec §7) — the production-shaped pacing.** The
  appender stalls whenever `append − commit > admission_budget` (default 4096 KiB
  = 4 MiB; the knob is in **KiB** so the fleet can sweep it — the p50 latency is
  dominated by window ÷ drain-rate, see Interpretation). These are **leader-local
  counters only** (the leader's own `append` and its own
  `commit`), so the identical pacing works **cross-host with no remote counter
  visibility** — which is exactly why it is the real fleet mechanism, and why the
  fleet `leader` role reports the same numbers as `local` mode.

### This closes the M2 fleet-pacing wedge

The M2 gate doc (`docs/benchmarks/uc2-m2-gate-2026-07-10.md`, "Methodology" and
its `leader_role` NOTE) flagged a **residual risk**: on the fleet the leader
cannot see follower `durable` counters, so M2's `leader` role paced its ring tail
against its own `sent` counter, which let `append` climb `cap/2` above the
follower frontiers — one dropped datagram could then leave a NAK gap already
scrolled out of the leader's ring (`serve_nak` Overrun → permanent wedge, since
replay sessions are M4). M2 could only avoid that with "generous buffers," and
named admission-control-vs-commit as the M3 fix. **That fix is here.** Pacing
against the **commit** counter (a leader-local quantity that, by the commit
definition, never runs ahead of the slowest quorum-durable follower) bounds
inflight to the admission budget on *every* node identically — no remote counters,
no `sent`-vs-`durable` skew, no wedge. The M2 residual risk is **resolved.**

To be precise about *which* wedge this closes: admission-vs-commit bounds inflight
relative to the certifying **quorum's** frontier (in a 3-node cluster, the
*faster* follower — commit is the 2nd-of-3 order statistic), not relative to every
follower. A single *minority-slow* follower can still fall more than one ring
behind and land in the documented **M4 replay-session envelope** (`serve_nak`
Overrun for that one follower); that is expected and handled in M4. What is closed
here is the **M2 quorum-path stall** — the leader's tail can no longer outrun the
frontier that actually certifies commit, so the quorum path never wedges.

Journal dirs are on ext4 under `/home/claude` (never `/tmp`, which is RAM-backed
tmpfs on this box), and `UC2_M3_MAX_BYTES` caps the appended stream so the
bounded run fits the disk. The journal root is `rm -rf`'d after the run; `df`
returned to the 48 GiB baseline.

## Interpretation

**Verdict: FAIL on the p50-latency bar** (43 ms ≫ 1 ms); **the throughput bar is
cleared** (734 232 committed/s ≥ 400 000). The verdict is FAIL because the gate
requires *both* bars. Both outcomes are exactly what the sandbox physics predict,
and neither is a code signal — the fleet is where the real gate is decided.

- **p50 = 43 ms is dominated by admission-window queueing, with the ext4 fsync
  floor as a smaller stacked term — and the window is a config knob, not
  architecture.** Decompose it:
  - **Queueing term (DOMINANT): window ÷ drain-rate.** With a 4 MiB admission
    window and **96 B frames** (64 B payload + 32 B header), up to **~43.7 K**
    messages (4 MiB ÷ 96 B = 43 690) are inflight (`append − commit`) at once. A
    sampled message waits behind that whole quorum-durable backlog, so its
    latency ≈ window ÷ the rate the quorum drains it. At the ~70 MB/s durable
    rate seen here, 4 MiB ÷ 70 MB/s ≈ **59 ms** of full-window wait; the observed
    p50 43 ms ≈ 0.7× of that (the window is not always full). **This term does
    NOT shrink when fsync gets faster** — it is set by the window size and the
    drain rate.
  - **fsync floor (smaller, stacked): one `fdatasync`.** The commit frontier
    advances only when a follower's `fdatasync` returns; on ext4 `/dev/sda1`
    that is ~ms per block — a hard lower bound of one such fsync per message,
    stacked under the queueing term.
  - **The knob moves the latency — proven by the 64 KiB run above.** Shrinking
    the window 4 MiB → 64 KiB collapsed p50 43 ms → 11 ms and, on this 4-core
    box, dropped committed/s 734k → 58k (fewer inflight frames also starve the
    pipeline here). That p50 still equals window ÷ drain-rate
    (64 KiB ÷ 5.5 MB/s ≈ 12 ms ≈ observed 11 ms) is the direct evidence the term
    is the admission queue, not an irreducible pipeline property.
  - **Why the fleet target requires a window SWEEP, not the default.** Faster
    NVMe fsync (tens of µs) lowers the floor term but does **not** collapse the
    queueing term: at the gate-floor 400k msg/s (38.4 MB/s) a 4 MiB window queues
    4 MiB ÷ 38.4 MB/s ≈ **109 ms**. To hit ≤ 1 ms the window must be small — a
    ~64 KiB window (≈ 680 frames ≈ **0.9 ms** at 70 MB/s) is in range. **So the
    fleet run MUST sweep the admission window (e.g. 64 / 256 / 1024 KiB) and
    evaluate the ≥ 400k-committed/s-AND-p50-≤-1 ms bar at the BEST point of that
    sweep — never at one default.** Reading the bar at a single 4 MiB window would
    risk a FALSE architectural halt under the spec's "miss badly → stop and
    re-diagnose" rule over what is a config knob, not the pipeline.

- **committed/s clears 400k here, but that is incidental, not the gate.** 734k/s
  on loopback reflects a heap→loopback→heap→ext4 software path with no wire and
  small payloads; it is not a claim that the fleet will hit 400k (the fleet adds
  real RTT and real NVMe, and removes core-sharing). The gate is the fleet run —
  this number just shows the pipeline is not throughput-broken in the software
  path.

- **The M3 correctness invariants all hold — this is what the smoke run
  validates:**
  - **`overruns == 0`** — no NAK gap ever scrolled out of the leader's ring; the
    admission window held the leader's tail at or below the quorum-durable
    frontier the entire run. This is the direct validation that
    admission-vs-commit pacing closes the M2 wedge (above).
  - **The commit drain completes** — the run only reaches the final report after
    the leader's **commit** counter reaches the stream end (732 065 312 B), i.e.
    every one of the 7 625 679 counted messages is quorum-fsync'd. The 300 s
    drain-stuck assertion never fired.
  - **The real commit invariants (pinned by `uc2_net/tests/commit.rs`, NOT
    inferred from the progress lines).** The per-second lines print f1's durable
    rate only, so they cannot witness an absolute quorum invariant — the harness
    quorum tests do. Two facts define commit:
    - **commit ≤ the leader's own durable.** The tracker caps the order statistic
      at `own`, so the leader never commits a byte it has not itself fsync'd.
      `commit_reaches_end_and_followers_learn_it` asserts `commit ≤ durable` at
      the converged point.
    - **commit = majority-of-3 durability.** Commit is the quorum-rank (2nd of 3)
      largest of {own durable} ∪ follower reports, so every committed byte is held
      durably by the leader **and at least one follower**. It therefore tracks the
      *faster* follower and may **lead the slower one's durable by design** — it
      is NOT "≤ every follower's durable." The tests pin the corollaries:
      `minority_failure_does_not_stall_commit` (commit reaches the end with one
      follower silent — leader + the live one suffice);
      `quorum_loss_stalls_commit_cleanly` (commit stays at 0 with both followers
      silent — own durable alone never commits, no phantom commit);
      `forged_and_stale_reports_cannot_move_commit` (an unknown-source or
      stale-term report cannot raise the frontier — guarded by
      `append_pos_unknown_source` + the demux stale-term drop).
    On this run `inflight = append − commit` stays pinned at the 4 MiB admission
    budget — a rate observation that the window governs and commit keeps pace, not
    the invariant proof.
  - **`flow_stalls == 0` — the quorum flow window never clamped the sender.** The
    sender's flow limit is the **256 MiB** follower buffer; the 4 MiB admission
    window is ~64× smaller, so the leader's `sent` never approaches the flow
    ceiling and flow control never fires. Pacing here is entirely the admission
    window; the throughput ceiling is fsync + core-sharing, not the flow window.

- **Drain-inclusive accounting, honestly applied.** The headline divides
  7 625 679 msgs by the full 10.39 s (load + commit drain), not by the 10 s append
  window; the `UC2_M3_MAX_BYTES=2 GB` cap did not bind (732 MB appended in the
  window), so the run is time-bounded and the extra 0.39 s is the tail commit
  drain, correctly inside the denominator.

## Fleet (3 × c6id, real LAN) result — the GO/NO-GO

_**Not yet run.** This is the decisive measurement: one node per 16-vCPU c6id
host over a real NIC, journals on local NVMe (tens-of-µs fsync), where the
core-sharing, loopback, and ~ms-ext4-fsync artifacts above **do not apply** and
the spec §9 bars (**≥ 400 000 committed/s AND p50 ≤ 1 ms, fsync on**) are the real
measurement. It runs with the maintainer via the fleet `leader`/`follower` roles
(commit and the latency samples are leader-local, so the leader-role console is
the real cross-host verdict — no remote counters needed, unlike M2).

**Run instruction: SWEEP the admission window before drawing any conclusion.**
Because p50 is dominated by window ÷ drain-rate (Interpretation), the fleet run
must sweep the admission window — e.g. `admission_kib` = **64 / 256 / 1024** — and
the spec §9 bar (**≥ 400 000 committed/s AND p50 ≤ 1 ms**) must be evaluated at
the **best point of that sweep**, never at one default. A single 4 MiB-window run
that misses p50 is NOT a signal to halt: at 400k msg/s a 4 MiB window queues
~109 ms by construction. Only if the bar is missed *at the best sweep point* does
the spec's "miss badly → stop and re-diagnose" rule apply.

**Binding gotcha (do this before the run):** each follower must bind its
**concrete NIC IP** (never `0.0.0.0`), so its outbound source address exactly
matches the address the leader has configured for that follower. If a follower
binds the wildcard, its AppendPosition datagrams can leave with a source address
the leader does not recognise, every report is dropped at the membership guard,
and — since own durable alone never commits — commit never advances. This
presents as a **total stall** (throughput pinned at 0) rather than an error; the
tell is a climbing `append_pos_unknown_source` on the leader console.

**The M3 go/no-go decision — continue to M4–M6, or stop and re-diagnose — is made
THERE, on the best point of that sweep, and will be appended here when
performed.**_

## Fleet result — 2026-07-11, 3 × c6id.2xlarge: **GO**

**THE GO/NO-GO GATE IS PASSED.** Best sweep point (256 KiB admission window):
**2,881,511 committed/s at p50 0.946 ms / p99 1.132 ms, fsync on** — 7.2× the
≥400k bar, inside the ≤1 ms bar. Even the smallest window clears both bars
with 2.6× headroom on latency. M4–M6 proceed.
